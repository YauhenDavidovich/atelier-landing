Outcomes

What you will be able to do

By the last lesson you will have taken a canvas from a blank ground to a finished, signed painting — and you will know the reason behind every stage of it.

Build a painting in stages

Ground, block-in, masses, accents — in an order that keeps the canvas alive.

Read value before colour

Value is how light or dark a colour is — get it right and the picture holds from across the room.

Mix clean colour

A limited palette, warm and cool pairs, and mixes that stay luminous instead of going to mud.

Handle paint with intent

Thick to thin, edge control, and the brushwork that gives an impressionist surface its energy.

Compose a subject

Choose a crop, place the focal point, and decide what the painting leaves out.

Critique your own work

Find the stage where a painting went wrong, so you stop repeating the same mistake.

Curriculum

The eleven lessons

Block one · lessons 1–2

Materials and the first marks

Paints, brushes, grounds and what is genuinely worth buying. Toning a canvas, holding the brush, the first honest marks.

You leave with: a set up workspace, a toned canvas and no fear of the blank surface.

Block two · lessons 3–5

Light, shadow and value

Seeing a subject as large masses of light and dark, squinting as a tool, and the block-in that decides the whole painting.

You leave with: two monochrome studies that read correctly from three metres away.

Block three · lessons 6–9

Colour that stays alive

A limited palette, warm against cool, greys that glow, and how a shadow takes the colour of the light around it.

You leave with: mixing charts you keep, and studies in full colour without mud.

Block four · lessons 10–11

The final canvas

Composing your own subject and carrying it through every stage, filmed start to finish alongside you.

You leave with: one finished painting worth hanging, and the method to repeat it.

Questions

Before you enrol

I have never held a brush. Is this too advanced?

No. The first block starts with materials and the first mark. What it asks of you is the practice between lessons, not prior experience.

What language is the course in?

English, with English subtitles on every lesson. Critique is written and recorded in English.

What will I need to buy?

A limited palette of six oil colours, four brushes, a palette knife and a few small canvases. The full list with EU, UK and US suppliers arrives with your access email.

Can I start later?

Yes. Enrol now and name a start date that suits you; the three months of access begin from that date.

How does the critique actually work?

You photograph your painting and upload it. Ksenia paints corrections over the image on a tablet, marks where the drawing or the value went wrong, and records the reasoning as audio.

What if it is not for me?

Write within 14 days of starting and you get the full amount back, no questions asked.

Can I use acrylics instead of oils?

The method transfers, but the lessons are filmed in oil and the drying times differ. If you paint in acrylic, come to the live sessions with questions.
